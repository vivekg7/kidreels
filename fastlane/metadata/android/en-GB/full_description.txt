Kid Reels looks and swipes like the short-video reels apps children already love, but every reel is a tiny learning game.

Made for children who will not eat or sit still without reels: keep the swipe, swap the content for games that teach, and get a gentle, built-in end to screen time.

WHAT YOUR CHILD PLAYS
• Counting, adding and taking away, with matchsticks under the numbers
• Times tables shown as dot arrays, number patterns, which is more
• Even and odd, square numbers, and prime numbers with a sieve to find them
• English ABC and Hindi वर्णमाला: "A for Apple", "अ से अनानास", and first-letter puzzles
• Shapes whose corners count themselves, 3D solids to spin with a finger
• Angles to drag open and see named, curves that draw themselves (ages 7 to 12)
• Telling the time, colours, big and small, odd one out, patterns
• One-square sudoku, with pictures for the youngest
• Animal sounds and animal homes
• Memory games: the cup shuffle, pairs, copy the order, what's missing
• Words: opposites, kinds of word, plurals, rhymes and Hindi मात्राएँ
• Science: photosynthesis, the water cycle and more, and the parts of a computer
• Mazes, from 3 × 3 at age 3 to 11 × 8 at 12
• Drag-and-drop games: the missing piece of a picture, shapes into their holes, food for each animal, counting into a basket, sorting
• Reasoning puzzles in the style of the Navodaya (JNVST) entrance test: odd figure out, series, mirror images, analogies, paper folding
• Picture cards for the youngest: animals, birds, vehicles, fruits, vegetables, body parts, things at home. Every picture says its name when tapped.

HOW IT KEEPS THINGS HEALTHY
• You set a daily time limit and a bedtime. Near the end only easy reels appear, then a calm goodbye reel says it is time to eat and play, or to sleep.
• An easy reel always follows a hard one; puzzles skipped come back a little easier, and any puzzle can start over.
• No streaks, points or badges to chase. A wrong tap is never called wrong: the voice says why it is not the answer and gives a hint.
• Settings open only after a grown-up holds a button for 3 seconds.
• Every few months, it asks you whether your child is ready for the next age.
• A note on the last seven days: time played, puzzles, what your child does well at, finds hard, often swipes past and likes most.
• Want just the alphabet today? Show one topic at a time, in order.

FOR BROTHERS AND SISTERS
• Up to 6 children on one phone, each with their own animal, age, languages, time limit and progress.
• "Who's playing?" shows each child's animal, so even a toddler finds their own.
• Once a child's time is up, switching to a sibling needs a grown-up.

FOR INDIAN FAMILIES
• Every line in up to three languages (English and Hindi now, more coming)
• Spoken aloud in the main language; Hindi letters in Hindi
• Ages 2 to 12, chosen by you

THREE LOOKS
Classic (dark, laid out like familiar reels apps), Candy (bright and bubbly) and Calm (soft and simple).

PRIVATE BY DESIGN
No ads, no tracking, no account and no internet permission: nothing leaves the phone. Free and open source (MIT).
