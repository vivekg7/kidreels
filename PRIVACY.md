# Privacy

Kid Reels collects nothing.

- **No network access.** The app does not declare the `INTERNET` permission, so Android
  will not let it open a connection. Every reel is generated on the phone.
- **No accounts, ads, analytics, crash reporting or tracking** of any kind, and no
  third-party SDKs that could do these.
- **What is stored on the phone**, in the app's private storage, for each child profile:
  the name the parent typed (it can be left blank), the animal and colour picked for it,
  the parent's settings (languages, age, look, time limit, bedtime, check-in interval,
  slow speech), today's minutes used, where each alphabet is up to, how often each topic
  was liked, per-age counts of puzzles tried and answered first time (shown to the parent
  in settings and at the age check-in), and for each of the last 14 days the minutes
  played and per-topic counts of puzzles and likes (for the weekly note in settings;
  older days are deleted). For the whole phone it also keeps the language picked for
  the app's own screens. A birthday is never asked for, and the name never
  leaves the phone. Android's own backup may copy this file to your Google account or a
  new phone if you have backup turned on; the app itself sends it nowhere.
- **Speech:** words are handed to the text-to-speech engine installed on your phone (for
  example Google's Speech Services) to be spoken. That engine is a separate app with its
  own privacy policy; with the voice installed it works offline. "Install voice" only
  opens the engine's or the system's voice settings.
- **Crash reports:** if the app crashes, a report is kept on the phone: the app and
  Android versions, the phone's make and model, the time, and the error's stack trace.
  Nothing about the child is in it. The app cannot send it; settings shows it to a
  grown-up, who can copy it to send by email or on GitHub, or delete it. Only the latest
  is kept.
- **Permissions:** none.
